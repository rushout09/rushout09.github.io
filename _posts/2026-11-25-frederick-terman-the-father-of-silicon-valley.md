---
title: "Frederick Terman, the Father of Silicon Valley"
---

I was reading Steve Jobs' biography and listening to Acquired's episode on Lockheed Martin when one name came up in both places: Frederick Terman.

People often think Silicon Valley happened because it happened to attract exceptionally smart people, or because of technological advances driven by WWII.

But much of it was deliberately built by Frederick Terman, often called the father of Silicon Valley.

After WWII, he did three things:

1. **Place:** Made 700 acres of Stanford land available for technology companies to set up offices and experiment.
2. **Talent:** Recruited some of the best professors and researchers from across the country to Stanford.
3. **Capital:** Made it easier for research to move out of universities and into companies through industry partnerships and technology transfer.

He basically brought the three ingredients of an innovation ecosystem together.

Companies that came because of Terman:

- **Hewlett-Packard:** started in 1939 by his students Bill Hewlett and David Packard, with his encouragement.
- **Varian Associates:** the first company to lease land in the Stanford Industrial Park, in 1951.
- **Lockheed, General Electric and Eastman Kodak:** early tenants of the park.
- **Fairchild Semiconductor:** a later tenant of the park.
