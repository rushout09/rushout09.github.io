---
title: "The Story of Vaikunth and Its Gatekeepers"
---

Vaikunth is the eternal abode of Lord Vishnu. It is considered the ultimate destination of souls. Loosely translated, 'Vai' means without and 'Kunth' means anxiety.

But Vaikunth is not necessarily a place without desires, aspirations or things to do. It is a place without the anxiety of achieving them, the fear of losing what you have, or the suffering that comes from attachment to outcomes.

It is considered a place one reaches after crossing seven concentric gates, conquering lust, anger, greed, attachment, arrogance, envy and ego.

There is an interesting story about two loyal and mighty gatekeepers, Jay and Vijay, who stood guard outside Vishnu's abode at the final gate.

One day, the Four Kumaras visited Vaikunth to see Lord Vishnu. Although they were ancient, spiritually realised, mind-born sons of Lord Brahma, a divine boon had kept them in the physical form of naked five-year-old boys.

They had easily conquered desire, anger, greed, attachment, pride and envy, allowing them to walk past the first six gates unhindered.

However, when they arrived at the seventh and final gate, Jay and Vijay stopped them from entering. Seeing four young, naked boys, they failed to recognise the spiritual stature of the Kumaras and refused them entry.

The Kumaras were furious. Having transcended worldly distinctions of age and appearance, they considered the gatekeepers' behaviour an act of arrogance. They cursed Jay and Vijay to be born on Earth, away from Vaikunth, in the mortal realm.

Jay and Vijay were devastated. Having served Lord Vishnu for eternity, the thought of being separated from him was unbearable.

When Lord Vishnu learned of the incident, he appeared before the Kumaras. Although he acknowledged that his gatekeepers had made a mistake, the curse could not be reversed.

He then offered Jay and Vijay a choice. They could either take seven births on Earth as devotees of Vishnu or take three births as his enemies, eventually being killed by Vishnu himself.

Unable to bear the thought of staying away from their Lord for seven lifetimes, they chose the latter.

In their first birth, they became Hiranyaksha and Hiranyakashipu, extraordinarily powerful demons who threatened the cosmic order. Vishnu took the forms of Varaha and Narasimha to defeat them.

In their second birth, they became Ravana and Kumbhakarna, the mighty brothers of Lanka. Vishnu incarnated as Lord Rama to defeat them.

In their third and final birth, they became Shishupala and Dantavakra, powerful kings who were ultimately killed by Lord Krishna.

Having completed their three births, Jay and Vijay were finally reunited with Lord Vishnu and returned to Vaikunth.

Across their three births, Jay and Vijay took different forms, each representing different human weaknesses: the arrogance of Ravana, the inertia of Kumbhakarna, the ego of Hiranyakashipu and the envy of Shishupala, among others. Each was eventually defeated by an incarnation of Vishnu, until they finally returned to Vaikunth.

Perhaps that is what makes the idea of Vaikunth so fascinating. It is not about giving up desires, ambitions or the pursuit of perfection. If anything, reaching it requires immense strength, discipline and mastery over oneself. You can aspire for everything, pursue your goals with all your might and push the limits of what you can achieve, without being consumed by the anxiety of achieving them.
